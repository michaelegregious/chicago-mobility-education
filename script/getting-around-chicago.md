# Getting Around Chicago

**Mobility Education Video — Working Script v0.1**

## Scene 1 — A City in Motion

| VIDEO | AUDIO / NARRATION |
|---|---|
| Morning in Chicago. Neighborhood streets coming to life. A parent walking a child to school. A cyclist unlocking their bike. A bus picking up passengers. An elevated train passing overhead. A driver pulling out of a parking space. | **NARRATOR:** Chicago. The City of Big Shoulders. |
| Quick cuts of people moving around the city in different ways. Downtown, neighborhoods, parks, commercial streets. | Every day, millions of us are getting somewhere. To work. To school. To the grocery store. To see friends and family. |
| A person driving to work, then later walking from their parked car. A parent cycling with a child, then riding the bus on another day. | One of the great things about Chicago is that we have choices about how we get around. We can walk, bike, take the train, ride the bus, or drive. And most of us use more than one of these ways of getting around. |
| A wide shot of a busy Chicago intersection, with people traveling in several different ways. | No matter how we choose to travel, we all share the same streets. |

## Scene 2 — A City of Neighborhoods

| VIDEO | AUDIO / NARRATION |
|---|---|
| Montage of Chicago neighborhoods. Two-flats, bungalows, corner stores, apartment buildings, parks, and neighborhood streets. | Chicago is a city of neighborhoods. Every neighborhood has its own character, its own people, and its own stories. |
| Children walking to school. Neighbors talking on a sidewalk. Someone carrying groceries across the street. A family sitting on their front steps. | Our streets aren't just ways to get from one place to another. They're where we live, work, play, shop, and spend time with our neighbors. |
| A driver traveling through an unfamiliar neighborhood. The camera shifts from inside the vehicle to the people walking and spending time along the street. | You might be passing through a neighborhood you've never visited before. But remember: wherever you are in Chicago, you're in somebody's neighborhood. Somebody's home. |
| A driver slows down near a playground. A child and parent cross the street. The driver waits patiently. | So whether you're close to home or on the other side of the city, treat every neighborhood with the same care you'd want people to show yours. |

## Scene 4 — Speed Matters

| VIDEO | AUDIO / NARRATION |
|---|---|
| A driver traveling along a Chicago neighborhood street. People walking dogs, waiting for buses, and crossing at intersections. | **NARRATOR:** When we're driving, one of the most important choices we make is how fast to go. |
| A speedometer showing 30 mph. A Chicago speed limit sign. | On most Chicago streets, unless otherwise posted, the speed limit is 30 miles per hour. But a speed limit is a maximum, not a target. Sometimes, even 30 miles per hour is too fast for the conditions. |
| Split-screen animation of two vehicles traveling at different speeds. A pedestrian becomes visible ahead. Both drivers begin braking at the same moment, but the faster vehicle travels considerably farther before stopping. | The faster you're going, the less time you have to react, and the farther your vehicle travels before you can stop. |
| Animated comparison of pedestrian collision outcomes at different speeds. **PRODUCTION NOTE:** Use verified crash-risk statistics and cite the source on screen. | And speed doesn't just affect whether a crash happens. It also affects how serious that crash will be. Even relatively small increases in speed can dramatically increase the risk of serious injury or death. |
| A driver approaches a busy commercial street. People step out from between parked cars. The driver reduces speed and scans ahead. | Think about the streets you travel every day. There are people crossing, cars pulling out, buses stopping, and things happening that you might not expect. Driving at a safe speed gives you more time to see what's happening and respond. |
| A car slows near a school, then stops for a person crossing. | Remember, getting somewhere safely is more important than getting there a few seconds sooner. |

## Scene 5 — People Walking

| VIDEO | AUDIO / NARRATION |
|---|---|
| A montage of people walking in Chicago: commuters, children, seniors, people using wheelchairs, people pushing strollers, and people carrying groceries. | Walking is the most basic way we get around. Even if you drive almost everywhere, you're a pedestrian every time you get out of your car. |
| Overhead view of a typical Chicago four-way intersection without painted crosswalk markings. An animated overlay highlights the extensions of the sidewalks across the street. | Here's something many Chicagoans may not realize: **a crosswalk doesn't have to be painted on the street to be a crosswalk.** At many intersections, the crosswalk is legally there whether you can see painted lines or not. |
| A pedestrian enters an unmarked crosswalk at an intersection without traffic signals. Vehicles approaching from both directions stop. | **Illinois law requires drivers to STOP for pedestrians crossing at marked and unmarked crosswalks when there are no operating traffic signals.** If a pedestrian is on your half of the roadway, you must stop. If they're approaching from the other half and are close enough to be in danger, you must stop then, too. |
| Overhead view of the same intersection. A pedestrian crosses from one side. The approaching vehicles stop and remain stopped as the pedestrian crosses. | This applies to drivers approaching from either direction. Don't try to squeeze through ahead of someone who's crossing. Give them the time and space they need to cross safely. |
| Driver's-eye view approaching an intersection. The camera highlights the roadway ahead, both sidewalks, and people approaching the corners. The driver adjusts speed and prepares to stop. | **Good drivers are always looking ahead.** A useful driving technique is to scan about 12 to 15 seconds down the road, while also watching the sidewalks and intersections around you. Look for people who may be preparing to cross, even where there are no signs or painted crosswalks. By anticipating what's ahead, you give yourself more time to slow down and stop safely. |
| A driver comes to a complete stop before the crosswalk. A pedestrian looks toward the vehicle and begins crossing. | A crosswalk is a place for people to cross the street. Whether it's painted or not, whether there's a sign or not, drivers need to recognize it and respect it. |
| A pedestrian crosses at a signalized intersection. A turning driver waits until the pedestrian has cleared the vehicle's path. | At intersections with traffic signals, drivers turning across a crosswalk must yield to pedestrians who are lawfully crossing. A green light doesn't give you permission to turn through someone who's walking. |
| A pedestrian crosses a street away from an intersection. The approaching driver slows to avoid a collision. | Pedestrians have responsibilities, too. Outside a crosswalk, they generally must yield to vehicles. But drivers still have a responsibility to exercise care and avoid hitting someone, even when that person isn't crossing where they should. |
| Return to the unmarked intersection. A pedestrian crosses safely while drivers wait. | **Remember: crosswalks don't need paint, and stopping for people crossing isn't just a courtesy. It's the law.** |

## Scene 6 — See What You Can't See

| VIDEO | AUDIO / NARRATION |
|---|---|
| Driver's-eye view traveling along a street lined with parked cars. A person is partly hidden between two vehicles. | One of the biggest challenges when driving in a city is that you can't always see what's happening around you. |
| An overhead animation reveals the driver's field of vision. Parked cars, a delivery truck, and a bus create blind spots. | Parked cars, trucks, buses, buildings, and other vehicles can block your view. And just because you can't see someone doesn't mean they aren't there. |
| A driver approaches a crosswalk where a delivery truck obstructs the view of the sidewalk. The driver slows and checks carefully. | When your view is blocked, slow down. Look for clues that someone might be crossing or preparing to cross. And give yourself enough time to respond. |
| Split-screen showing the driver's perspective and the perspective of a pedestrian trying to cross from behind parked cars. | Remember that sightlines work both ways. If you can't see a pedestrian, they may not be able to see you either. |
| Camera returns to a driver approaching a busy intersection at a cautious speed. | Good driving isn't just reacting to what you can see. It's anticipating what you might not be able to see yet. |
